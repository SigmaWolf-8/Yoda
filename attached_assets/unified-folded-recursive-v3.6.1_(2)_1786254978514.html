§6   General Principles

  1. Jurisdiction precedes obligation. No duty arises from a citation. A statute governs conduct only where the actor, the vessel, the cargo, or the tort falls within its reach.
  2. Flag, coastal and port State jurisdiction are distinct. The flag State has primary jurisdiction on the high seas (UNCLOS art. 92); the coastal State exercises defined powers in the territorial sea, contiguous zone and EEZ (arts. 2, 17, 19, 33, 56); the port State exercises control on entry (art. 218; Paris and Tokyo MOUs).
  3. Sovereign immunity is categorical. Warships and government ships on non-commercial service are immune from the jurisdiction of any State other than the flag State (UNCLOS arts. 32, 95, 96). Immunity does not excuse compliance; it removes the forum.
  4. Limitation is a shield, not a cap on duty. Global and package limitation regimes limit recovery; they do not reduce the standard of care and are broken by personal act or omission committed with intent or recklessly with knowledge (LLMC art. 4; Hague-Visby art. IV r. 5(e)).
  5. Safety obligations are non-derogable by contract. Neither charterparty nor bill of lading may lower a statutory seaworthiness, manning, or occupational safety standard.
  6. Measurement must be traceable. Every quantity carries a unit, a reference standard, and a stated uncertainty. Dimensionless indices (GT, NT) are never treated as masses.
  7. The law of armed conflict applies from the first shot, irrespective of the legality of the conflict. Jus in bello is independent of jus ad bellum (Geneva Conventions, Common Art. 2; AP I preamble).
  8. Distinction is absolute; proportionality is a judgement. Attacks on civilians and civilian objects are prohibited without exception (AP I arts. 48, 51(2), 52(1)). Proportionality applies only to incidental harm from a lawful attack on a military objective.
  9. ROE constrain, never enlarge. Rules of engagement may forbid what LOAC permits; they can never permit what LOAC forbids.
  10. Command responsibility runs upward. A commander is liable for crimes of subordinates he knew or should have known of and failed to prevent or punish (AP I art. 86(2); Rome Statute art. 28; Crimes Against Humanity and War Crimes Act, SC 2000, c. 24, s. 5).
  11. Record integrity is itself a duty. An error preserved for consistency is still an error. Corrections are ledgered, not silently absorbed.
  12. Status is not conferred by assertion. A document does not become a filing, a claim, or a warship by describing itself as one.
