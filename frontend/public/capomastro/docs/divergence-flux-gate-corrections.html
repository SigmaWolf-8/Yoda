Divergence–Flux Gate — Corrections

This supplement states the precise global and local implications of the divergence theorem. It accompanies the divergence identity in Section XIX of the Unified Sovereign Compendium. The original sealed documents are unchanged.

1. Assumptions and orientation

Let V be a bounded open regular volume in ℝ³ with piecewise-smooth boundary ∂V. Require F ∈ C¹(U; ℝ³) on an open neighborhood U of the closure of V. Let n be the outward unit normal on ∂V, and let dS⃗ = n dS.

The divergence theorem gives:

∭V ∇⋅FdV = ∯∂V F⋅ndS = ∯∂V F⋅dS→

Reversing orientation changes the flux’s sign, but does not change whether it is zero. The displayed equality with the divergence integral uses outward orientation.

2. Global gate — one volume

∯∂V F⋅ndS =0⇒ ∭V ∇⋅FdV=0

For a particular volume V, this says only that the total integral of the divergence is zero. It does not imply that ∇ · F = 0 pointwise inside V. Positive and negative divergence can cancel.

3. Counterexample — cancellation without pointwise vanishing

Take F(x, y, z) = (x², 0, 0) on the unit ball V = B₁(0). Then:

∇⋅F= 2x≢0
∯∂V F⋅ndS =∭V 2xdV=0

The integrand 2x is odd in x and the ball is symmetric under x ↦ −x, so its integral vanishes. The divergence itself is nonzero wherever x ≠ 0.

4. Local gate — every interior ball

Under the assumptions above, the precise local statement is:

∇⋅F≡0 in V⇔ ∯∂Br(p) F⋅ndS =0

The flux condition must hold for every ball of positive radius whose closure lies inside V. Equivalently, it may be required for every admissible subvolume W contained in V. A single volume does not suffice.

Continuity of ∇ · F supplies the pointwise conclusion at every p ∈ V:

∇⋅F(p)= limr↓0 1|Br(p)| ∯∂Br(p) F⋅ndS

Here |Bᵣ(p)| is the ball’s volume. By the divergence theorem, flux divided by volume is the average divergence over the ball. As the ball shrinks to p, that average tends to ∇ · F(p). If every such flux is zero, the limit is zero. Conversely, identically zero divergence yields zero flux through every such ball by the same theorem.

5. Terminology

A vector field with ∇ · F = 0 is divergence-free or solenoidal. “Incompressible” applies when F is a velocity field, expressing the local volume-preserving condition; it is not a general physical interpretation of an arbitrary vector field.

6. Computational gate — scope of the conclusion

Zero net flux on one domain verifies only the global balance. Checking finitely many subvolumes does not, by itself, prove pointwise divergence-freedom.

A finite numerical check must be reported as such, with its numerical tolerance. It must not be presented as an exact proof of the universal local condition.
