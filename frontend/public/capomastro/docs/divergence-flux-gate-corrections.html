Divergence–Flux Gate — Corrections

This supplement states the precise global and local implications of the divergence theorem. It accompanies the divergence identity in Section XIX of the Unified Sovereign Compendium. The original sealed documents are unchanged.

1. Assumptions and orientation

Let V be a bounded open regular volume in ℝ³ with piecewise-smooth boundary ∂V. Require F ∈ C¹(U; ℝ³) on an open neighborhood U of the closure of V. Let n be the outward unit normal on ∂V, and let dS⃗ = n dS.

The divergence theorem gives:

∭V ∇⋅FdV = ∯∂V F⋅ndS = ∯∂V F⋅dS→

Reversing orientation changes the flux’s sign, but does not change whether it is zero. The displayed equality with the divergence integral uses outward orientation.

2. Global gate — one volume

∯∂V F⋅ndS =0⇒ ∭V ∇⋅FdV=0

For a particular volume V, this says only that the total integral of the divergence is zero. It does not imply that ∇ · F = 0 pointwise inside V. Positive and negative divergence can cancel.

3. Counterexample — cancellation without pointwise vanishing

Take F(x, y, z) = (x², 0, 0) on the unit ball V = B₁(0). Then:

∇⋅F= 2x≢0
∯∂V F⋅ndS =∭V 2xdV=0

The integrand 2x is odd in x and the ball is symmetric under x ↦ −x, so its integral vanishes. The divergence itself is nonzero wherever x ≠ 0.

4. Local gate — every interior ball

Under the assumptions above, the precise local statement is:

∇⋅F≡0 in V⇔ ∯∂Br(p) F⋅ndS =0

The flux condition must hold for every ball of positive radius whose closure lies inside V. Equivalently, it may be required for every admissible subvolume W contained in V. A single volume does not suffice.

Continuity of ∇ · F supplies the pointwise conclusion at every p ∈ V:

∇⋅F(p)= limr↓0 1|Br(p)| ∯∂Br(p) F⋅ndS

Here |Bᵣ(p)| is the ball’s volume. By the divergence theorem, flux divided by volume is the average divergence over the ball. As the ball shrinks to p, that average tends to ∇ · F(p). If every such flux is zero, the limit is zero. Conversely, identically zero divergence yields zero flux through every such ball by the same theorem.

5. Terminology

A vector field with ∇ · F = 0 is divergence-free or solenoidal. “Incompressible” applies when F is a velocity field, expressing the local volume-preserving condition; it is not a general physical interpretation of an arbitrary vector field.

6. Computational gate — scope of the conclusion

Zero net flux on one domain verifies only the global balance. Checking finitely many subvolumes does not, by itself, prove pointwise divergence-freedom.

A finite numerical check must be reported as such, with its numerical tolerance. It must not be presented as an exact proof of the universal local condition.

7. Application in the ledger

The Conservation Gate workspace implements discrete boundary reconciliations, NMT/XRP valuation checks, reserve-cell observations, and balanced write-off journals. The application displays their shared status on the Ledger, Statements, Collections, Codex, Seal, and Compendium pages.

These are accounting checks with declared quantities, units, boundaries, periods, and evidence. They are not a proved isomorphism between accounting, entropy, fluid flow, and law. For a conserved physical quantity, accumulation and any internal source terms must also be included in the continuity equation. Zero divergence alone does not prove zero entropy production.

Net inward flow = inflows − outflows
Outward flow = outflows − inflows
Residual = closing − opening − net inward flow − declared non-flow adjustments

A zero residual reconciles the declared period and inputs only. It neither locates a leak nor verifies every internal account. The sealed workbook has no complete opening/closing external-flow event history for GL-9-0001 through GL-9-0008; absent evidence remains unknown, not zero.

8. Energy-bridge valuation corrections

Under the supplied model, 1 NMT represents 1 kWh, with an electricity assumption of 0.10 USD/NMT and hardware assumption of 0.02 USD/NMT. Cost basis is NMT × 0.12 USD. Recorded settlement value is XRP quantity × recorded USD/XRP price.

Valuation gap = NMT × 0.12 − XRP × price (USD)
Reference price = 200000 × 0.12 = 24000 USD/XRP

For 200000 NMT and 1 XRP, a price of 23999.50 USD/XRP gives a 0.50 USD valuation gap. A price of 0.50 USD/XRP gives a 23999.50 USD gap. Those are different scenarios. Comparing a USD/XRP price to 0.12 USD/NMT is dimensionally invalid; the percentage price deviation uses 24000 USD/XRP as its reference denominator.

Zero valuation gap means valuation parity under the declared assumptions, not physical losslessness. No XRP is mined, minted, transferred, or repriced by this audit. Positive gaps produce a debit in the supplemental journal; gains produce a positive credit, not a negative debit.

9. Evidence and sealing limits

Saved observations retain their inputs, computed results, evidence reference, timestamp, and SHA-256 predecessor chain. Every append validates the inputs, recomputes the result, verifies the existing chain, and checks any generated journal for exact debit-credit equality. Failed observations are retained as failures; they are not suppressed or converted to a passing seal.

The database rejects updates and deletions of audit entries. Keep an independently retained chain head to detect later replacement. Hash chaining is not an external signature, evidence of ownership, or proof that an operator’s declaration is true. Write-offs retain the evidence: no asset or historical journal is silently removed.

Reserve coverage is an inequality (reserves ≥ liabilities), not an assertion that every flux is zero. Finite reserve observations, balanced journals, and mod-9 residues do not certify continuous backing or compliance with GAAP, OSHA, the UCC, or the GENIUS Act.
